The *muzhi* (墓誌), or inscribed epitaphs, and *muzhiming* (墓誌銘), or entombed epitaphs, developed from the Han tradition of inscribing *bei* (碑), or steles, to commemorate the deceased. These forms of epitaphs developed during the Northern and Southern Dynasties period (~300–581 CE) in the aftermath of the disintegration of the territory previously held together under the Eastern Han (25–220 CE). Even prior to the death of the last emperor of the Han in 220 CE marking the official end of the dynasty, late Eastern Han was marked by growing regionalization and the rise of networks of powerful magnates.[^1]With this growth of powerful families came corresponding markers of status, including elaborate tombs and commemorative steles which then expanded into extended epitaphs. As a contemporary record of individuals, their families, their careers, and the language by which they were described in both life and death, these epitaphs offer invaluable sources of information on early medieval Chinese society[^2]and the individuals who occupied this time and place.

## Formula of medieval epitaphs

1. Title of Deceased
1. Identity of Deceased (name, region of origin, lineage, etc.)
1. Qualities of Deceased
1. Life/Career of Deceased
1. Date and Location (and circumstances) of Deceased's death
1. Date and Location (and posthumous honors) of Deceased's burial
1. Verses honoring Deceased

東魏驃騎大將軍開府儀同三司長廣郡開國公高公（湛）妻茹茹公主閭氏（叱地連）墓誌銘</p>

**Translation**

The Princess was named Chídìlián. She was the granddaughter of the Rouran khan (Anagui) and the daughter of Khan Anluochen. Her family’s origin was distant and wide, their earthly achievements continuous within the powerful northern wilderness, their names echoed over past dynasties. Their prosperity and achievements are found within numerous records, overflowing the pages of history. 

The princess’s body contained many generations of auspicious signs. She was genteel and endowed with the harmony of warm and gentle *qi*. Her mastery of the four feminine virtues was pure and perfect. She had the auspiciousness of the six ranks, her eminence had the solemnity of the inner quarters, such that her virtue spread to the clans and families throughout the nation. The princess respected her elders, consulted the *Odes* and the *Histories*, and always put others before herself. She was trustworthy and amiable in both thought and deed. The women of the imperial court all followed her example. She was a striking figure amidst a crowd of beauties. 

The Wei Emperor (Eastern Wei puppet emperor Yuan Shanjian 元善見 （524-552 CE)), following the Way of Imperial Rule, reflected on the world and the power and influence of its hegemons and rulers. Both in the North and South, all accepted the laws of civilization, whereby foreigners were invited to the Imperial Court. The Rouran Khan admired and respected the virtue of the Wei Emperor who was a relative of his tribe by marriage. The khan therefore arranged for his granddaughter to be married as a peace bride, making her the consort of the Duke’s son. Upon the arrival of this remarkable person, the princess carried with her the demarcations of moral standards and the glad tidings of a glorious age, daily renewing her virtue. One would expect such a glorious person to enjoy long life stretching into old age, but these are hollow words as she died suddenly. 

On the seventh day of the fourth month in the eighth year of the Wending period (May 8, 550 CE), she died in Jinyang. She was thirteen years old (twelve by Western standards). On that year, on the thirteenth day of the fifth month, she was buried on the southern bank of the Fu River, in the tomb of King Xianwu of Qi (Emperor Jing of Wei). 

The emperor passed down an edict which stated, “The Dynasty-Founding Duke of Changguang’s wife the Rouran Princess of Neighborly Peace passed away suddenly. The noble shall heave a sentimental sigh at this. Arriving at the gates of merit with generations of virtue, her brilliance covered the court and commons. According to the traditions which governs the paying of Our respects, these fixed rules are fitting and excellent. We may pass down this Imperial edict that orders the construction of a mourning carriage in the Bing region, preparing it according to common custom and convention, with the proper rites.” 

Thereupon, we engrave on the stele of the Underworld, such that it shall hand down her merits and achievements forever.

**Original**

公主諱叱地連，茹茹主之孫，諳羅臣可汗之女也。源流廣遠，世緖綿長，雄朔野而揚聲，跨列代而稱盛，良以布濩前書，備諸歷史矣。公主體奕葉之休徵，禀中和之淑氣，光儀婉嫕，性識閑敏，四德純備，六行聿脩，聲穆閨闈，譽流邦族。若其尊重師傅，訪問詩史，先人後己，履信思順，庶姬以為謨楷，衆媛之所儀形。皇魏道映寰中，霸君威棱宇縣，朔南被教，邀外來庭。若主欽挹風猷，思結姻好，乃歸女請和，作嬪公子。亦既來儀，載閑禮度，徽音歲茂，盛德日新。方享遐期，永綏難老，與善徒言，消亡奄及。以武定八年四月七日，薨於晉陽，時年十三。即其年歲次庚午五月己酉朔十三日辛酉，葬於釜水之陰，齊獻武王之塋内。天子詔曰：長廣郡開國公妻茹茹鄰和公主，奄至喪逝，良用嗟傷。既門勳世德，光被朝野，送終之禮，宜優常數。可敕并州造輼輬車，備依常式，禮也。乃銘石壤陰，永傳餘烈。
